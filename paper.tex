
How the house predicted heat load value is created using the following three steps.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Estimate hourly house heating energy using a multivariate linear regression based on weather and recent load, as shown in Equation \ref{eq:house_predicted_regression}. Here $Q_\text{P, H}$ is the predicted hourly house heating energy, $b_0,\ldots,b_6$ are regression coefficients, $\Delta T = \max(0, \bar{T}_\text{set} - T_\text{O})$ is the average zone setpoint minus OAT ($T_\text{O}$), $\dot{V}_\text{W}$ is wind speed, $I_\text{S}$ is solar irradiance, $\bar{T}_\text{O,4h}$ is the four-hour average OAT, and $Q_{\text{P,H},t-1}$ and $\bar{Q}_\text{P,H,4h}$ are the previous hour and four-hour average house energy use (distribution-side measurements scaled to house level; see below).
    \item Define the scaling factor $R$ as shown in Equation \ref{eq:scaling_factor}, where $Q_\text{HP}$ is the thermal heat provided by the AWHP and $Q_{\text{m,d}}$ is the measured hourly distribution energy. Because of losses in the basement pipes, the regression is fit to $R\,Q_{\text{m,d}}$ each hour so that total scaled distribution energy matches total AWHP output over the monitoring period.
    \item Apply the fitted regression each hour to obtain $Q_\text{P, H}$, setting the value to zero if negative.
\end{itemize}


\begin{equation}
Q_\text{P, H} = b_0 + b_1 \Delta T + b_2 \dot{V}_\text{W} \Delta T + b_3 I_\text{S} + b_4 Q_{\text{P,H},t-1} + b_5 \bar{T}_\text{O,4h} + b_6 \bar{Q}_\text{P,H,4h}
\label{eq:house_predicted_regression}
\end{equation}


The regression coefficients in Equation \ref{eq:house_predicted_regression} are found by fitting the equation to all available cleaned hourly measurements so that the prediction matches $R\,Q_{\text{m,d}}$ as closely as possible overall. More precisely, they are chosen by ordinary least squares to minimize the sum of squared errors over the monitoring period. Coefficient values and standard errors for each house are given in Table \ref{tab:regression_results}.


\begin{equation}
R = \sum(Q_\text{HP}) / \sum(Q_{\text{m,d}})
\label{eq:scaling_factor}
\end{equation}
